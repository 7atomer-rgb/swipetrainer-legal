Parabolic move and capitulation patterns

A parabolic move is a stock racing higher with no pause, and capitulation is the mirror image: a rush lower as holders give up. Both are climactic, and both are famous for catching people on the wrong side.

What it looks like

The stock moves 30% or more in a short time, with each day pushing into new territory, and it ends the window sitting at the very edge of its range. Moves that stretch this far this fast tend to be climactic rather than sustainable, but timing the reversal is notoriously hard.

The rules we use

Many chart examples online are loose. In SwipeTrainer a parabolic / capitulation must pass a strict check before it is used:

  • A big, fast move: at least 30% in 30 days or less.
  • At the extreme: price sits in the top 15% of its range (parabolic) or the bottom 15% (capitulation).
  • New territory: the move reaches beyond anything seen earlier in the window, so it is not just a rebound.
  • Visible lead-in: the move starts well inside the chart, not at the left edge.

How traders use it

Some traders take profits into parabolic strength, others avoid shorting it because the move can keep going. For capitulation lows, many wait for signs that selling is exhausted. Both are high-risk situations, which is exactly why they make good practice material: the outcomes can go either way.

Common mistakes

  • Shorting a parabolic move just because it looks extended.
  • Catching a falling knife without confirmation.
  • Ignoring that momentum can persist far longer than expected.
  • Treating one example as a rule.

Frequently asked questions

What is a parabolic move in stocks?

A steep, accelerating rise with few pauses, often followed by a sharp reversal, though not always.

What is capitulation?

A fast, heavy drop as many holders sell at once. Some traders see it as an exhaustion low, but it can continue.

Is it safe to trade against a parabolic move?

It is risky. The move can keep going much longer than expected, which is why practising on real examples first is useful.
