Chart pattern cheat sheet: 8 patterns, their rules and measured odds

One page to compare the patterns traders watch. For each: what it is, the first rule we require, and how often it moved the expected way 10 trading days later in 2,312 stocks since 2015.

PatternWhat it isKey ruleMeasured odds
Bull and bear flagsA flag is a short, tidy pause after a sharp move.A real pole: a move of at least 12% in 3 to 8 days, built from several candles going the same way, not one big gap day.Bull flag: 51% as expected (400 ex.)
Bear flag: 42% as expected (122 ex.)
Trading range / horizontal channelA trading range, also called a horizontal channel or tunnel, is a stretch where a stock bounces between a floor and a ceiling instead of trending.A real band: the range is about 5% to 12% wide. Narrower is just noise, wider is a swing, not a channel.Trading range (tunnel): 52% up (1,123 ex.)
Accumulation (volume)Accumulation is a volume story.Quiet price: the stock moves 12% or less across the window, so there is no obvious trend.Accumulation (volume favours buyers): 50% as expected (2,346 ex.)
Accumulation (volume favours sellers): 46% as expected (1,222 ex.)
Volatility contraction (VCP)In a volatility contraction pattern each pullback is shallower than the one before.At least two pullbacks, and each one is at least 15% shallower than the last.Volatility contraction: 53% up (14,736 ex.)
Double top and double bottomA double top is two nearly equal highs with a dip between them, a sign the market tried a level twice and could not break through.Matching extremes: the two highs or lows are within about 2.5% of each other.Double bottom: 54% as expected (1,531 ex.)
Double top: 50% as expected (1,681 ex.)
Head and shoulders (and inverse)A head and shoulders is a reversal shape with three peaks: a left shoulder, a higher head, and a right shoulder at about the same height as the left.A prominent head: the head extends at least 4% beyond both shoulders.Inverse head and shoulders: 56% as expected (1,772 ex.)
Head and shoulders: 51% as expected (1,778 ex.)
Breakaway gapA breakaway gap is a stock opening far above or below the previous close, usually on news, with unusually heavy volume.A real gap: the open is at least 6% away from the prior close.Gap up: 50% as expected (4,887 ex.)
Gap down: 52% as expected (3,790 ex.)
Parabolic moves and capitulationA parabolic move is a stock racing higher with no pause, and capitulation is the mirror image: a rush lower as holders give up.A big, fast move: at least 30% in 30 days or less.Capitulation (sharp drop): 56% as expected (2,874 ex.)
Parabolic run-up: 51% as expected (7,747 ex.)

A random chart ended higher 53% of the time over the same window. "As expected" means higher for bullish patterns and lower for bearish ones. Past results, not a prediction; not investment advice. See the full results and method.

How to use this sheet

  • Start with the pattern's rules, not its name. If a rule fails, it is not the pattern.
  • Read the odds as a tilt, not a signal. Most sit within a few points of a random chart.
