Trading range (horizontal channel) pattern

A trading range, also called a horizontal channel or tunnel, is a stretch where a stock bounces between a floor and a ceiling instead of trending. It is one of the most common shapes and one of the easiest to misjudge.

What it looks like

Price keeps returning to roughly the same support (the floor, where buyers step in) and the same resistance (the ceiling, where sellers appear). The edges are flat, the stock crosses the middle again and again, and eventually it breaks out in one direction.

The rules we use

Many chart examples online are loose. In SwipeTrainer a tunnel (trading range) must pass a strict check before it is used:

  • A real band: the range is about 5% to 12% wide. Narrower is just noise, wider is a swing, not a channel.
  • Flat edges: the top and the bottom are level lines, not a rising or falling channel.
  • Alternating tests: at least two touches of each edge, taken in turn (top, bottom, top, bottom).
  • Back and forth: price crosses the middle of the range at least five times, so it is genuinely oscillating.
  • No hill or valley: no part of the window sits far from the middle, which would mean a trend in disguise.

How traders use it

Range traders buy near the floor and sell near the ceiling, and breakout traders wait for a decisive close outside the range, often with a jump in volume. The longer and cleaner the range, the more attention a break tends to get. A move that pokes outside and snaps back is a false breakout, and these are common.

Common mistakes

  • Mistaking a gentle uptrend or downtrend for a range.
  • Trading the middle of the range, where the odds are poor.
  • Assuming the first break is real. Wait for confirmation.
  • Forgetting that a range ends. Never assume it will hold forever.

Frequently asked questions

What does a horizontal channel tell you?

That buyers and sellers are in balance at the current levels. It does not say which side will win, only that a decision is coming.

How many touches make a trading range?

Two touches on each side is the minimum, and more is stronger. The touches should alternate between the floor and the ceiling.

What is a false breakout?

A move beyond the edge of the range that quickly returns inside it. They trap traders who act on the first poke.
