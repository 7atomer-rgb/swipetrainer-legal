Breakaway gap pattern

A breakaway gap is a stock opening far above or below the previous close, usually on news, with unusually heavy volume. It is one of the loudest signals on a chart, and one of the hardest to call.

What it looks like

The stock closes at one price and opens the next session at a very different one, leaving a hole on the chart. Gaps backed by real volume suggest that new information is changing the picture. Gaps on thin volume are often ordinary noise.

The rules we use

Many chart examples online are loose. In SwipeTrainer a breakaway gap must pass a strict check before it is used:

  • A real gap: the open is at least 6% away from the prior close.
  • Volume confirms it: volume that day is at least 50% above its recent average.
  • Fresh: the gap happened within the last few weeks, so it still matters on the chart.
  • With lead-in: there are at least eight earlier candles on the chart, so you can see what the stock was doing before the jump.

How traders use it

Some traders trade gaps for continuation, betting the move keeps going in the same direction, while others fade them, expecting the gap to fill. Both happen. That is why gaps are so useful to practise on: the same-looking gap can lead to opposite outcomes, and the clues are often in the volume and in what came before.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming every gap fills, or that no gap ever does.
  • Ignoring the earnings or news context.
  • Chasing the open with no plan.
  • Treating a thin-volume gap like a high-volume one.

Frequently asked questions

What is a breakaway gap?

A large price gap, often on news and heavy volume, that can mark the start of a new move. It differs from smaller, routine gaps.

Do gaps always get filled?

No. Some fill quickly, some never do. Volume and context help, but nothing is guaranteed.

What is the difference between a gap up and a gap down?

A gap up opens above the prior close, a gap down opens below. Both can continue or reverse.
